Definitions
Active
In CelesTrak's list of active satellites, its judgement of which payloads are operational.
Active + inactive
Every payload in orbit regardless of operational status, including dead satellites.
Age
Years since launch: this year minus the launch year.
All objects
Every catalogued object still in orbit: all spacecraft, plus rocket bodies (spent upper stages), debris (fragments from break-ups, collisions and launches) and objects the catalogue lists as unknown or has not yet given a type. CelesTrak's active list is of working satellites, so ACT-ONLY with ALL-OBJ adds almost nothing to ACT-ONLY with ALL S-CRFT: only the odd active satellite the catalogue has not yet typed as a payload. For a rocket body or debris, country and owner are those GCAT records for that object.
All spacecraft
Every payload still in orbit (not yet re-entered).
Country
GCAT's state for the object: the country (or intergovernmental organisation, such as ESA or the EU) responsible for it, in practice where its owner is based. Not where it was built or launched from. Satellites launched by the Soviet Union keep that code (SU), separate from Russia (RU).
Eccentricity
How stretched an orbit is: 0 for a circle, approaching 1 for a long thin ellipse. Worked out here from perigee and apogee as (apogee radius − perigee radius) ÷ (apogee radius + perigee radius), radii measured from the Earth's centre.
Excl SpaceX
(Table row; the EX SPACEX button does the same for the charts.) Leaving out every payload with SpaceX among its owners in GCAT (any of its SpaceX codes, including Starlink's operator). For a payload not yet in GCAT, membership of CelesTrak's Starlink list is used instead.
GEO
Geostationary orbit: mean altitude within ±200 km of 35,786 km, where an orbit takes one day, so a satellite over the equator appears fixed in the sky.
> GEO
Mean altitude more than 200 km above GEO: almost all retired GEO satellites moved up to "graveyard" orbits, plus a few science missions.
GEO longitude
Where a geostationary satellite sits over the equator, in degrees east or west, worked out from its latest element set and carried forward to today by its drift. Accurate to about a degree.
HEO
Highly Elliptical Orbit: eccentricity above 0.25, whatever the altitude. Molniya and Tundra orbits, transfer orbits and long looping science orbits.
Inclination
The angle between the orbit and the equator: 0° circles the equator, 90° passes over the poles, above 90° travels against the Earth's rotation (as sun-synchronous orbits do, at about 97°–99°).
LEO
Low Earth Orbit: mean altitude 450 km to 2,000 km. Most satellites, including the broadband constellations and most Earth observation.
Log scale
An axis on which each step is ten times the last (1, 10, 100, 1,000 …), so very large and very small counts fit on one chart. Read the printed counts for exact values.
Manufacturer / bus
Who built a payload, as GCAT records it (the first maker listed, as a legal entity -- one company can appear under more than one), and the satellite platform ("bus") it is built on.
Mean altitude
The average of perigee and apogee.
MEO
Medium Earth Orbit: mean altitude 2,000 km to 35,586 km. Navigation systems (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou) sit at about 19,000–23,000 km.
Mission category
GCAT's category for what a payload is for, grouped: communications; imaging (optical and radar); navigation; signals & EW (signals intelligence and electronic warfare); weather & Earth science (including geodesy); science & astronomy; technology demo; crewed & stations; other. Where GCAT lists several, the first is used.
NORAD number
The NORAD catalogue number given to every tracked object. It links the catalogue to GCAT.
Owner
The organisation GCAT records as owning a payload, as a legal entity rather than a parent group: SpaceX's Starlink satellites belong to SpaceX (Seattle), and one company can appear as more than one entity (OneWeb is two). Where GCAT lists several owners, the first is used, so each payload counts once. Owner is not operator: GCAT does not record who runs a satellite day to day.
Passive object
A payload with no working systems by design: calibration spheres (GCAT category CAL) and anti-satellite test targets (TARG).
Payload
A catalogued object launched to do a job: a satellite, probe, crewed vehicle or station module. The catalogue's other objects, rocket bodies and debris, are never counted here.
Perigee / apogee
The lowest and highest points of an orbit, as altitudes above the Earth's surface.
Satellites
All spacecraft except crewed space stations and the vehicles that visit them, and passive objects. Small satellites released from a station count as satellites.
Sector
GCAT's class for each payload: A academic, amateur or non-profit; B commercial; C civil government; D defence, military or intelligence. A payload with no class of its own takes its owner organisation's. A few are dual-class (e.g. TerraSAR-X, civil and commercial) and are counted under the first letter GCAT gives. Unknown: not yet in GCAT, or no class recorded.
Space station / visiting vehicle
GCAT category SS: ISS and Tiangong modules, Progress, Shenzhou, Tianzhou, Cygnus, Dragon and the like. A visitor GCAT has not catalogued yet is recognised by its membership of CelesTrak's stations list.
VLEO
Very Low Earth Orbit: mean altitude below 450 km (ESA and DARPA's definition; some sources use 400 or 500 km). Orbits this low decay quickly without regular propulsion.
How the table and charts are built
Chart groups

The charts are in groups, chosen with the second set of buttons: LOCN (what is where -- orbit type, inclination, altitude, the GEO belt, age) and COMMERCIAL (who owns it -- sector, mission, country, owner, manufacturer). DEBRIS (where the debris is, what broke up, rocket bodies) and COMMS (downlink bands). The group showing is lit white; ON-ORBIT opens on LOCN.

Data sources

Orbits and status: the NORAD satellite catalogue, taken as OMM element sets from CelesTrak and Space-Track (a few from SatNOGS). It gives each object's current orbit, whether it is a payload, and whether it has re-entered. CelesTrak's lists supply which payloads are active, which are space stations and visitors, and which are Starlink. Refreshed every 12 hours.

Ownership, country and category: GCAT, the General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects by Jonathan McDowell (planet4589.org), refreshed weekly. GCAT's own orbits are the orbit each object was first placed in (Starlinks at about 280 km before they raise to about 470 km), so they are never used.

The two are matched by NORAD number. All figures are recalculated after every refresh of either source.

Population

Every object in the catalogue that has not re-entered; payloads only, unless ALL-OBJ is selected. The ACT-ONLY / ACT +DEAD and SAT ONLY / ALL S-CRFT / ALL-OBJ buttons then narrow or widen it (the second cycles through its three settings), and apply to the table and every chart alike. INC SPACEX / EX SPACEX applies to every chart; the table needs no such button, as it shows ALL and EXCL SPACEX side by side.

Pie charts

Orbit regime pie: the regime table's counts (its ALL row, or EXCL SPACEX with EX SPACEX on) as shares of the whole. Payloads with no current orbit are left out, as from the table.

Sector pie: every payload in the population by GCAT sector class (see Sector, above), so its total is the nationality chart's. Thin slices are hard to see; the legend gives every count and share.

Orbit regime table

Each payload is placed from its current orbit in two steps: shape first, so an eccentricity above 0.25 is HEO whatever its altitude; then mean altitude for every other orbit, into VLEO, LEO, MEO, GEO or > GEO.

ALL counts every payload in the population; EXCL SPACEX leaves out SpaceX's. Each % row is a share of its own row's TOTAL. A few payloads with no current orbit on file are not placed; their number is in the reconciliation below and in the orbit-type pie's tooltip.

Nationality chart

Payloads counted by GCAT country. The 12 countries with the most are shown individually, plus the European Space Agency wherever it ranks; the rest are combined as Other, together with the payloads not yet in GCAT (about 3%), which have no country.

The United States is split into Starlink (CelesTrak's Starlink list, or a STARLINK name) and the rest. Copernicus satellites, such as the Sentinels, are GCAT's EU, not ESA. A jointly owned satellite counts once, under the single state GCAT gives it.

Log scale, because the largest bar is hundreds of times the smallest.

Inclination chart

Payloads counted by current inclination, read directly from each one's latest element set, in 5° bands from 0° to 180°. Each band includes its lower edge but not its upper (50° is in 50–55°); 180° goes in the last band. Empty bands are kept, so the axis is the same whatever the filters.

Payloads with no published elements have no inclination and are left out; hover over the total in the chart's title bar for how many. Most are long-dead satellites in high orbits no longer routinely tracked.

Log scale on the count axis, because the busiest bands (Starlink's shells, sun-synchronous orbits) hold thousands and the quietest one or two.

Owners chart

Payloads counted by owner, one bar per owner, largest first. The 40 largest owners are shown, with names at the same size as the axis numbers. A final Other bar holds every owner not shown, together with the payloads with no owner on file, so the bars add up to the whole population; the title bar gives how many owners are shown out of how many in all, and hovering over its total gives the share they hold. Each bar's share of the whole population is written inside it. Hover over a bar or name for the owner's full name, country and count.

Log scale on the count axis, because the largest owner (SpaceX, when included) holds thousands and the smallest shown a few dozen.

LOCN charts

Active vs inactive by orbit type: every object in orbit, active or not by CelesTrak's list, by regime -- this chart ignores ACT-ONLY, as it compares the two. Inclination by orbit type: the inclination histogram with each 5° band split by regime. Altitude: mean altitude in 25 km bands below 2,000 km, log scale, showing the constellation shells. Altitude vs inclination: one dot per 25 km × 1° cell, sized by count and coloured by the sector most common there. GEO belt: geostationary objects in 2° bands of longitude, by sector (graveyard orbits not included). Age: years since launch, 30 and over together, by regime.

COMMERCIAL charts

Sector and mission pies side by side. Sector by country: the 12 countries with most objects, each bar split 100% by sector, its total at the right. Owners: as before. Manufacturers and satellite buses: the 20 most common of each, log scale. Payloads in orbit by year: payloads in orbit at each year end since 1957, by sector, from GCAT's launch and decay dates -- only the SpaceX button applies, as there is no history of operational status.

These charts, apart from the pies, nationality and owners, are counted when shown, from one row per object in orbit recalculated after every catalogue or GCAT refresh.

Country and owner tree

The COMMERCIAL group ends with a directory-style tree: countries with their owners beneath, largest first, click a country to open or close it. Each shows its 6 largest owners and the rest together; the 15 largest countries are listed.

DEBRIS charts

Every object in orbit -- the status and kind buttons don't apply (debris is never active, and isn't a payload); the SpaceX button does. By type and orbit: payloads, rocket bodies, debris and unknown in each regime. Altitude: every object below 2,000 km in 25 km bands, by type -- where the debris is. Debris history: bars for the pieces created each year, by the date each broke off; a line, on its own right-hand axis, for the pieces in orbit at each year end (created so far less re-entered so far) -- from GCAT's record of all debris ever catalogued. Debris by inclination: 5° bands, log scale. Rocket bodies by country and by launch vehicle (top 15). Debris by origin: the 20 objects with most debris still in orbit, named from GCAT's record of which object each piece broke off (so a rocket stage that fragmented shows as the stage, not as the satellite it launched), with its launch year.

COMMS charts

All three buttons apply. Downlink band pie, then each band split by mission and by orbit type. A satellite's band is mostly inferred -- from its constellation, or estimated from its type and orbit -- and only one is recorded, though most use several; about 1,000 have an actual frequency from SatNOGS.

Limitations

Orbits are at most about 12 hours old, ownership at most about a week. "Active" is CelesTrak's judgement and can lag satellites that fail or retire quietly. The 0.25 eccentricity threshold for HEO and the 450 km VLEO ceiling are common conventions, not standards. Categories (stations, calibration spheres, targets) are GCAT's own.

Reconciliation of numbers of spacecraft and satellites
Where totals appear

Several places give a total, and they need not agree: the title bars of the two pie charts, the nationality chart and the inclination chart, and the TOTAL column of the orbit regime table; and the toggle buttons change what is counted. All of them start from the same population, every payload in the catalogue that has not re-entered, narrowed by the buttons; the differences come from what each one can place, and from the buttons themselves.

Nationality chart

The full population. Every payload has a bar to go in: those not yet in GCAT (about 3%, mostly recent launches) have no country and are counted under Other rather than left out. So this is the largest of the chart and table totals, and the reference the others are measured against.

Regime table TOTAL

The population less payloads with no current perigee or apogee on file, which cannot be placed in a regime; their number is given in the figures above. They are a handful of objects (at the last check, six) known only from a SatNOGS element set, which carries no perigee or apogee: mostly military reconnaissance satellites whose orbits the official catalogue does not publish, plus the Aditya-L1 solar observatory, which is not in Earth orbit at all.

With EX SPACEX selected, compare the charts with the table's EXCL SPACEX row: the table does not change with that button, as it always shows both.

Pie chart totals

The orbit regime pie's total is the regime table's TOTAL; the sector pie's is the full population, the nationality chart's, with payloads not in GCAT as Unknown.

Inclination chart total

The population less payloads with no inclination on file (hover over the chart's total for how many). The catalogue gives these a perigee and apogee but publishes no current orbital elements, so there is no inclination: mostly long-dead satellites in high orbits no longer routinely tracked, and some recent launches. It is a different, larger group from the one missing from the table: the few objects with no perigee or apogee do have an inclination, so they are in this chart but not the table.

Owners chart total

The full population, the nationality chart's: the largest owners, then Other, which holds the remaining owners and the payloads with no owner on file (the same payloads counted as Unknown in the sector pie).

Satellites vs all spacecraft

SATELLITES is ALL SPACECRAFT less crewed space stations and the vehicles visiting them, and passive objects. At the last check that was 65 payloads in all: 40 calibration spheres, 16 station modules and visiting vehicles, and 9 anti-satellite test targets. Only 35 of them are active, as most calibration spheres and targets are not.

Active vs active + inactive

ACT-ONLY counts CelesTrak's list of operational payloads; ACT +DEAD adds every payload still in orbit that is not on it: about 3,100 dead or retired payloads, spread across every regime and including nearly all of those in the graveyard orbits above GEO.

With vs without SpaceX

EX SPACEX removes every payload SpaceX owns, almost all Starlink, from both charts. The charts' totals then equal the table's EXCL SPACEX row (less any payloads with no current orbit), which applies the same rule.

Other published counts

Counts elsewhere often differ from these, for reasons of definition rather than error: some count only satellites believed operational by their own research, some include rocket bodies or exclude cubesats, some count only satellites with a confirmed owner. Most are also snapshots taken at a different time. Classified satellites whose orbits are not published are missing from the public catalogue altogether, so no count based on it, including this one, includes them.
