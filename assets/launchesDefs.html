Definitions
Launch attempt
One rocket launch intended to reach orbit or beyond, counted once however many payloads it carried, whether it succeeded or failed.
Orbital / deep-space
GCAT's launch codes O (to Earth orbit) and D (beyond: the Moon, planets, the Sun's orbit). Suborbital tests (codes N and X) are not counted.
Failed launch
A launch GCAT records as a failure (second letter of its code F): the vehicle did not deliver its payloads to the intended orbit.
Payload
A catalogued object launched to do a job: a satellite, probe, crewed vehicle or station module. Not the rocket stages or debris a launch also leaves behind.
Payload launched
A payload that reached orbit (or beyond), counted in the year it was launched. Payloads lost in a launch failure (GCAT status N, never reached orbit) are not counted.
Sector
GCAT's class for each payload, in the same colours across the site: A academic, amateur or non-profit (blue); B commercial (earth); C civil government (amber); D defence, military or intelligence (red). A payload with no class of its own takes its owner organisation's.
Re-entry
A catalogued object coming back down: re-entering the atmosphere (GCAT status R), deliberately de-orbited (D) or landed (L: crew capsules, returned vehicles), counted in the year it came down.
Rocket body / debris / other
Rocket bodies are spent stages; debris is fragments from break-ups, collisions and launches; other is components such as fairings and adapters that were catalogued.
EX SPACEX
For launch attempts: launches carried out by SpaceX (Falcon 1, Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, Starship) are left out. For payloads and re-entries: objects SpaceX owns are left out -- almost all Starlink satellites and Falcon upper stages.
Successful launches
The LCH-OK setting (LCH-ALL: every attempt) counts only launches GCAT records as successful (second letter of the launch code S). It applies to the launch-attempts, launches-and-payloads and by-country charts; the payloads-launched chart already counts only payloads that reached orbit, and re-entries are not launches.
Start and end year
The years shown on every chart, typed into the control panel; they default to the full range, from 1957 to this year. A new value is used when you press Enter or leave the field, if it is a year in that range and the start is no later than the end; otherwise the previous value is put back. Totals in the title bars cover the years shown.
Year to date
The current year is still running, so its figures are counts so far. In the launch-attempts chart its point is shown on its own, not joined to the line.
How the charts are built
Data source

GCAT, the General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects by Jonathan McDowell (planet4589.org), refreshed weekly: its launch log for launches, and its satellite catalogue for payloads and decays. The payload and re-entry figures are recalculated after every refresh; launch attempts are counted each time the chart is drawn.

Launch attempts chart

Distinct launches per calendar year since 1957, from GCAT's launch log (one row per payload, so each launch is counted once), orbital and deep-space, successes and failures. Hover over a point for the year's count and how many failed.

Launches and payloads chart

Launches and the payloads they carried per year on the left axis, and payloads per launch -- their ratio -- on the right axis (dashed line). All three come from GCAT's launch log, so they agree with one another; payloads here include any lost in a failed launch, so the count is slightly above the payloads-launched chart's. With EX SPACEX, SpaceX launches and everything they carried are left out.

Launches by country chart

The launch attempts per year, stacked by the country GCAT gives each launch vehicle -- so each year's bar adds up to the launch-attempts chart's point. Europe combines France (Arianespace), ESA, ELDO, Italy, Germany and the UK. GCAT codes Rocket Lab's Electron as New Zealand, where it launches from, although the company is American. The Soviet Union and Russia are kept separate. Other: Iran, Israel, North and South Korea, Brazil, Australia and the Cayman Islands.

Payloads launched chart

Payloads that reached orbit, by launch year, stacked by sector. Starlink is counted within commercial; EX SPACEX takes it out.

Re-entries chart

Catalogued objects that came back down, by the year of their decay date, stacked by type. Objects that came down docked or attached to another (e.g. a cargo ship on a station) are not counted again, and departures to deep space are not re-entries. Debris too small to be catalogued is not included, and some decay dates are approximate (the year is normally right).

Limitations

Every classification -- success or failure, sector, object type, how an object left orbit -- is GCAT's own. The current year is a count to date.
